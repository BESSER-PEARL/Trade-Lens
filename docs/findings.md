| 1999-01 to 2026-06 (the full history) | August, €0.16 bn below trend on average (−14%) | October, +€0.12 bn (+10%); March close behind (+9%) | the Overview's seasonal note, the Trends page by default, the trend finder's reply |
| 2015-01 to 2026-06 (the CN8 era) | August, €0.16 bn below trend (−13%) | March, +€0.11 bn (+9%) | the Trends page with From = Jan 2015 |

August is the weakest month on both spans (the summer break is a likely explanation, not something the data shows); the strongest month moved from October to March in the last decade, and the two are close on the full history. Read with `/trends/analyze?flow=EXPORT&freq=M&start=1999-01&end=2026-06` (or `start=2015-01`), `seasonal_pattern`. Months of 2026 are provisional and will be revised, and STL estimates are least stable at the ends of the series. The Explorer shows the seasonally adjusted series for the world total, each chapter and each partner's total (the monthly data loaded).

## 8. Smartphones: a code from 2022, a flow that is not new

CN8 85171300 (smartphones) only exists from 2022: the 2022 edition of the HS split heading 8517.12 and gave
smartphones a code of their own (8517.13). Until 2021 they were filed with other mobile phones under CN8
85171200, "telephones for cellular networks or for other wireless networks". So the 2025 jump is large against
2022 to 2024, but not unprecedented for mobile phones through Luxembourg:

| 2016 | €1,108.2 m | €1,024.5 m | 85171200 |

Read with `/insights/series?flow=EXPORT&freq=A&start=2015&end=2025&product=85171200` (and `product=85171300`,
`flow=IMPORT`), checked on 15 October 2026. The two codes are not exactly the same goods (85171200 also held
mobile phones that were not smartphones), so say "mobile phones" for the years before 2022. A year-long
pass-through of €1 bn to €2 bn has happened before (2015, 2016, 2019) and faded within a year or two, which is
why the forecast carries the "line stops / line continues" scenarios. The agent says this history when it
speaks about the smartphone line, and charts the two codes as one chained line with the change marked at 2022.
